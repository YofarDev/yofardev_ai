persona gets a body-language profile (gesture rate and choice, posture, motion amplitude, idle style)

### Wardrobe 2.0

3. **Bytes before art.** `avatar.riv` is 564,776 / 614,400 B. Measured (Task 2 survey, ±15 %): the
   wardrobe is ~368 KB (65 %) of the file; the four non-default hand skins are 70.6 KB of pure colour
   copies (the three gloves share geometry and differ by four colours); the sleeves are 108.8 KB for
   11 outfits × 2 arms, of which 46.6 KB is skin weights, and outfits of the same sleeve kind differ
   only by colour. So M10 first **dedupes** (Task 2a): one sleeve set per sleeve *kind* (long, short,
   bare, puff, glove, armor, stars) instead of per outfit, and one glove hand for black / metal /
   white (and the skin / stars hands if their geometry matches), with each outfit's colours keyed in
   its existing `top_*` / costume switch clip (`KeyFrameColor`). Gate: every outfit renders identical
   to M9 (≤ 1/255 renderer noise) at rest and at the gesture peak frames. **Only then** new art.
   **Budget line for M10: the avatar ends ≤ 564,776 B** (no net growth: the dedupe pays for the
   tints, the anchor and the signatures, and M11–M12 keep the ~49 KB reserve). If the dedupe saves
   less than the new art costs, the signatures shrink first (ruling 6).
4. **Rejected for the budget:** a lazily loaded `wardrobe.riv` (bones, skin weights, the head turn,
   the walk and the gesture keys all live in `avatar.riv`; a second file could only match the rest
   pose: the journey file's jacket coincides with the hand only at one frame) and `NestedArtboard`
   wardrobe pieces (a host bone moves a nested artboard rigidly but cannot skin its paths, and view
   models are flat on rive_native 0.1.11). Both are overturnable if a later runtime skins across
   nested artboards.
5. **Colour variants are data-bound tint fills (contract v13 `topTint`, `hatTint`).** Every painted
   shape of the tops (front, back, both sleeves, the outfit's base and shade shapes; never ink lines,
   seams, prints or skin) and of the hats gains a second `Fill` drawn over its own fill, blend mode
   **color** (hue and saturation from the tint, lightness from the garment, so the authored shading
   survives), whose `SolidColor` binds the AvatarVM colour. Default `00000000` (alpha 0) draws
   nothing: identical to M9. The tint's alpha is its amount. Costumes are not tinted (the app writes
   alpha 0 while a costume is worn). Task 2b proves the color blend on the CLI renderer and on
   rive_native (macOS contract test); if rive_native ignores it, fall back to multiply over a
   lightened tint and record it.
   - **Dart:** `AvatarTint` (`none`, `red`, `orange`, `yellow`, `green`, `teal`, `blue`, `purple`,
     `pink`, `grey`; core/models), its colours in `core/design/` (`WardrobeTints`, contrast-free:
     they are paint, not text). `Avatar` gains `topTint`, `hatTint` (default `none`), stored under
     the new map keys `topTint` / `hatTint`; a missing or unknown key reads `none`, so **every saved
     chat loads unchanged** (the legacy `top`/`bottom` key swap is untouched). The `change_avatar`
     tool gains `topTint` and `hatTint` (options from the enum). The December outfit rule keeps
     working (a tinted default outfit is no longer "the default": `shownWardrobe` compares tints too).
     The architecture test's ban on the substring "Outfit" stands (names use "wardrobe" / "tint").
6. **A signature move per costume (contract v13 `gestureId` += `signature`, appended).** One gesture
   value whose clip depends on the costume worn (each transition checks `gestureId` = signature and
   `costume` = X): **batman** a cape sweep (the arm sweeps across the chest on the arm rig, the cape's
   `Response` follows), **robocop** a stepped scan-and-salute (held keys, the `Scanner` sweeps fast),
   **soubrette** a curtsy with the apron's follow-through, **singularity** a levitation (the body
   rises 18 px, the aura swells, the laser flashes once). With `costume` none it is a 6-frame no-op
   that still emits `gesture_done`. Each ≤ 1.2 s, keys only on the rig (arm IK targets, body,
   `Response` nodes), ≤ 5 KB each. The app requests it once when a costume is put on (after the outfit
   switch), and the director may pick it as a persona gesture for a costumed avatar (ruling 9).
7. **Old chats.** Enum values are only appended, never renamed; new fields default; the stored map
   keeps its legacy keys. A test loads fixtures of every M9 stored avatar shape (with and without
   costume, legacy backgrounds) and finds them unchanged.
8. **Not in wardrobe 2.0 for M10:** new garments (the deduped bytes are the M11–M12 reserve), tinting
   glasses or costumes, per-garment pattern variants, a wardrobe UI (the LLM and `change_avatar`
   remain the way to dress; a picker is an M11 accessibility candidate).

### Persona body language

9. **`BodyLanguageProfile`, one per `ChatPersona`** (`lib/core/models/performance/body_language.dart`,
   pure const data; `normal` is the identity profile: today's behaviour exactly, so every existing
   test holds). Fields:
   - `gestureRate` (0.5–1.6): the auto-acting rules fire their gesture with this weight, and the
     director's cooldown is `gestureCooldown / gestureRate`;
   - `gestureBias`: a small ordered substitution table for auto gestures (e.g. doomer: a beat becomes
     `shrug`; coach: `thumbsUp`, `clap`; philosopher: `think`; psychologist: `nod`; geek: `count`,
     `thumbsUp`), applied deterministically from the sentence's index (no RNG beyond the director's
     seed); LLM gestures are never changed;
   - `posture`: offsets added to the solver's rest `lean` (−0.15–0.2), `pitch` (−0.25–0.1) and
     `roll` (−0.1–0.1) (the doomer slumps: head down, lean back; the coach leans in, chin up);
   - `motionScale` (0.6–1.4): the amplitude of prosody nods, tilts and brow accents in
     `HeadMotionSolver` / `FacePerformer`, and the `intensity` of AU accents (clamped as today);
   - `idle`: the ladder's thresholds scale (0.7 fidgety – 1.4 calm), the glance period scale, and the
     idle prop (`tablet` default; `newspaper` for the philosopher and the conservative).
   Values per persona are a table in the file with a one-line why each. Gesture **amplitude** of the
   keyed arm clips is not scaled (a keyed clip cannot be scaled history-free on 0.1.11 without
   wrapping every gesture in a blend state; overturnable, M11 candidate): amplitude lives in the
   procedural channels (head, face, lean) and in the choice of gesture.
10. **Where the persona comes from:** the current chat's `persona` (a chat started with a persona
    keeps it), streamed to the avatar domain the same way the mood is (`AvatarCueService` /
    `service_locator.dart`), never a cubit reading another cubit. A persona change applies at the
    next sentence (no posture snap mid-sentence: the solver eases posture over 600 ms; reduce motion
    applies posture without easing).
11. **Deterministic evidence, no LLM:** `tool/body_language_trace_test.dart` (`flutter test`, like
    the M2 face trace) runs one scripted reply (fixed sentences, cues and timings) through the real
    director + solver + face performer for each of the eight personas and writes a table (gestures
    played, mean / max lean, pitch, nod count, idle stage times) into `rive/review/m10/personas/`;
    one in-app capture (`WOW=persona`) plays the same scripted line as doomer, coach and philosopher
    for a contact sheet.

### Diegetic captions

12. **The avatar file exposes its mouth (contract v13 `MouthAnchor`).** A named `Node`
    `MouthAnchor` at the centre of the lips, parented so that it follows the head turn, pitch, roll,
    jaw, breath, lean and the walk (inside the head's turn pivot, not skinned). The app reads its
    world transform the way M9's `AvatarZoneHitGate` reads `HitHead`
    (`artboard.component(name).worldTransform`), once per frame after the avatar advances, and maps
    artboard → stage through the avatar's fit (`Fit.contain`, bottom centre) and the `StageCamera`
    transform. Missing component (an older file), web, or a non-finite value → no anchor.
13. **The bubble.** While the avatar speaks a sentence with captions on, a glass speech bubble
    (`GlassPanel` + `SpeechBubbleBorder`, `context.text` display type, `captionInk` on the scene's
    `glassTint`) sits beside the mouth: on the side with more room, its tail pointing at the anchor,
    max width 72 % of the stage, at most 4 lines (else the classic caption), clamped inside the safe
    area between the top chips and the tool chip / composer, never over the face (a keep-out box
    around the anchor). It follows the anchor smoothly (a critically damped 8 Hz follow, no jitter
    from per-frame noise below 1 dp), and re-lays out only per sentence. Contrast of text on the
    bubble ≥ 4.5:1 checked in a test for every scene.
14. **Fallbacks and accessibility.** The classic `StageCaption` shows instead when: no anchor
    (ruling 12), the anchor is off the stage or the camera is on `propInsert`, a journey or presence
    walk runs, the bubble would not fit (text scale ≥ 1.5 or > 4 lines), or a screen reader is on
    (`MediaQuery.accessibleNavigation`: the classic caption is one stable live region). **Reduce
    motion:** the bubble is placed once per sentence at the anchor and never follows (no per-frame
    movement), fades in `AppMotion.fast`, no slide. The caption toggle (tap to hide, `enabled`
    persisted) is unchanged; no new setting (overturnable: a "caption style" setting is an M11
    accessibility candidate).
